# Boda-to-Theatre: a prospective registry of road-traffic abdominal trauma in Kampala

> Example project for the plenary. It grows out of the clinical case *"Defying the Odds: Complex Hepatobiliary and Duodenal Trauma Following a Road Traffic Accident in Uganda"* (Matsiko, Kamyuka, Mwanje, Henry; Makerere University; Journal of the Global Health Academy, May 2026). The research design below is a proposed next step, not the authors' plan.

## From one case to a research question

- The case: a 25-year-old hit by a motorcycle, brought to hospital by boda-boda because no ambulance was available, BP 80/62, SpO₂ 86%. He survived liver, gallbladder, duodenal and hepatic-artery injuries through damage-control surgery and an improvised sphincterotomy with T-tube, because ERCP was not available.
- A single case shows what is possible. It cannot show how often patients die before reaching theatre, how long the delays are, or which improvisations work.
- Road-traffic crashes kill about 1.3 million people a year worldwide (WHO), and sub-Saharan Africa carries the highest death rates.

**Question.** For patients with blunt abdominal trauma after road-traffic crashes in Kampala, how do transport mode and time from injury to laparotomy relate to 30-day mortality, and how well does the Kampala Trauma Score predict who dies?

## What we will do (12 months)

1. **Set up (months 1–2).** Ethics (Makerere SOMREC) and UNCST registration; a one-page data form agreed with surgeons, ED and ICU staff at both sites.
2. **Enrol (months 2–11).** All adults with blunt abdominal trauma after a road-traffic crash; about 300 patients across two sites (estimate to be checked against admission logs).
3. **Data capture.** Research nurses fill a short offline form on a phone (KoBoToolbox or REDCap Mobile). An AI helper, run on de-identified records only, flags missing or impossible values each night and converts free-text operative notes into standard categories. No names or record numbers leave the hospital.
4. **Follow-up.** 30-day outcome by phone call.
5. **Analysis (month 12).** Delay–mortality relationship; KTS and TRISS calibration; case series of improvised damage-control techniques and their complications.

## Outcomes

| Type | Measure |
| --- | --- |
| Primary | 30-day mortality |
| Timing | Injury → arrival; arrival → FAST; arrival → laparotomy |
| Prehospital | Transport mode (boda-boda, private car, taxi, ambulance), referrals between facilities |
| Prediction | Kampala Trauma Score and TRISS discrimination and calibration |
| Surgical | Damage-control steps, improvised techniques, re-operation, biliary leak, ICU stay |
| Data quality | Share of complete records before and after AI checks |

## Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Patients who die before arrival are invisible | Record deaths in the ED and on arrival; later link to police or mortuary data |
| Missing times (no watches, no documentation) | Ask patient, family and boda-boda rider; record time bands, not exact minutes |
| Loss to 30-day follow-up | Two phone numbers at enrolment; community contact person |
| Privacy with AI tools | De-identified extracts only; data stay on the hospital or university server |

## Sustainability

The registry becomes the backbone of a Kampala trauma-quality programme. Results feed the national case for a prehospital system, and make the next grant (a trauma-systems intervention) much stronger. The case report is already the first publication.

## Ready-to-paste summaries

**One sentence.** A 12-month prospective registry at two Kampala hospitals to measure how boda-boda transport and delays to laparotomy affect survival after road-traffic abdominal trauma, and how well the Kampala Trauma Score predicts death.

**50 words.** In Kampala, injured patients often reach hospital by motorcycle taxi, and the delay to surgery is unknown. Building on a published case of survival against the odds, we will follow about 300 patients with abdominal trauma at two hospitals, measure every delay, test the Kampala Trauma Score, and document what improvised surgery achieves.

**Keywords:** road-traffic injury, blunt abdominal trauma, prehospital care, damage-control surgery, Kampala Trauma Score, trauma registry, Uganda
