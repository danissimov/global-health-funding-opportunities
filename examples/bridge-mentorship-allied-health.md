# Bridge: WhatsApp mentorship with an AI helper for early-career allied health professionals

> Example project for the plenary, combining ideas from three conference posters: the audiology transition study (*"From Classroom to Clinic"*, Chataika, University of Zimbabwe), WhatsApp telehealth that works (*AMAI*, University of Zimbabwe) and health-worker capacity building (*UnFold*, St. Stephen's Hospital, Uganda). It also uses the Global Health Academy's own model: GHA Dialogues, communities of practice and train-the-trainer faculty development (Journal of the Global Health Academy, May 2026). ## Problem

- New professions are growing fast: the University of Zimbabwe graduated its first audiology class in 2022 (poster).
- Graduates often start work alone in district hospitals, with little supervision or mentorship. That is the gap the "Classroom to Clinic" study explores.
- Seniors are few and busy. Mentoring has to fit on a phone, in short bursts.

## Research question

Does a WhatsApp-based mentoring network with an AI case-structuring helper improve early-career allied health professionals' clinical confidence and retention, and is it feasible for busy senior mentors?

## What we will do (12 months)

1. **Set up (months 1–2).** Recruit 36 juniors and 6 mentors across 3 countries; add 3 Nuvance specialists as visiting mentors. Agree privacy rules: no names, photos or record numbers in any chat.
2. **Run the network (months 3–11).**
   - Weekly WhatsApp case questions within each group.
   - A monthly 60-minute online case conference, run like the GHA Dialogues.
   - The AI helper, used on the junior's own phone with de-identified text only, drafts the case question ("age range, key findings, what I tried, what I need") and a 5-line reflective log after each case.
3. **Evaluate (months 3–12).** Mixed methods: surveys at baseline, 6 and 12 months; interviews with 12 juniors and all mentors, building on the "Classroom to Clinic" qualitative work.

## Outcomes

| Type | Measure |
| --- | --- |
| Confidence | A validated professional self-efficacy scale, baseline vs 12 months |
| Retention | Still in post and in the profession at 12 months |
| Feasibility | Messages per week, mentor minutes per week, case-conference attendance |
| Quality | Proportion of case questions that are complete and answerable (rated blind) |
| Safety | Privacy breaches; advice later judged unsafe |

## Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Patient data shared in chats | Written privacy rules; AI helper strips identifiers; monthly audit of a sample of messages |
| Mentors drop out | Honoraria; protected time letter from each department; 1 back-up mentor per country |
| Low engagement | Short weekly prompts; certificates of CPD hours from the professional council |
| Three ethics approvals | Start with one site; add others in month 3 |

## Sustainability

Professional councils (for example MRPCZ in Zimbabwe) can recognise participation as CPD. After the pilot, the university runs the network as part of internship support; the running cost is data bundles and a few hours of mentor time.
